Peptide Science: HPLC, COA & Testing Methods

10. What is HPLC purity testing?
HPLC — High-Performance Liquid Chromatography — is an analytical testing method that separates a compound mixture by molecular properties to measure the concentration of the target peptide versus any impurities. The result is expressed as a percentage. A ≥99% HPLC purity result means 99% or more of the sample is the intended compound, with ≤1% consisting of degradation products or synthesis byproducts. It is the industry standard for verifying research peptide quality.
Target KWs: what is HPLC purity testing, HPLC purity meaning, HPLC peptide test
11. What is a Certificate of Analysis (COA)?
A Certificate of Analysis (COA) is a formal document from an independent laboratory confirming the quality and identity of a specific production batch. For research peptides, a standard COA includes: HPLC purity percentage, LC-MS mass spectrometry identity confirmation, batch lot number, endotoxin testing results, and the laboratory’s accreditation details. A COA from an independent third-party lab (not the manufacturer’s own testing) is the gold standard for verification.
Target KWs: certificate of analysis COA, peptide COA explained, what is a COA
Internal CTA: Internal: 'View our full COA Library → /certificates'
12. What is lyophilization?
Lyophilization — also called freeze-drying — is a process that removes moisture from a peptide solution by freezing it and then applying a vacuum to convert the ice directly to vapor, leaving behind a stable, dry powder. Lyophilized peptides are significantly more stable during shipping and long-term storage than liquid formulations. Most research peptide vials are lyophilized for this reason. Reconstitution with bacteriostatic water is required before use in laboratory applications.
Target KWs: lyophilized peptide definition, what is lyophilization, freeze dried peptide
Internal CTA: Internal: 'Use our Peptide Reconstitution Calculator → /peptide-calculator'
13. What is bacteriostatic water?
Bacteriostatic water (BAC water) is sterile water containing 0.9% benzyl alcohol, which acts as a preservative by inhibiting the growth of bacteria, yeast, and mold. It is the standard diluent for reconstituting lyophilized research peptides because the preservative properties extend the stability of the reconstituted solution significantly compared to sterile water alone. Most reconstituted peptides remain stable for 2–4 weeks when stored at 2–8°C using BAC water.
Target KWs: bacteriostatic water peptides, BAC water explained, what is BAC water

23. How should I store unmixed peptides?
Store lyophilized (unreconstituted) research peptide powder in a freezer at −20°C or below for long-term stability. Lyophilized peptides are resilient in dry form — brief room-temperature exposure during transit does not cause significant degradation. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles, which can compromise structural integrity over time. Once received, transfer to the freezer promptly.

Use our free Peptide Reconstitution Calculator at /peptide-calculator for exact measurements. 25. How long do reconstituted peptides stay stable?
Once mixed with bacteriostatic water, most reconstituted research peptides remain stable for 2 to 4 weeks when stored upright in a refrigerator at 2–8°C. Bacteriostatic water extends shelf life significantly by inhibiting microbial growth via its 0.9% benzyl alcohol content. Never freeze a reconstituted peptide solution. If your solution becomes cloudy, discolored, or shows visible particulates, discard it and reconstitute a fresh vial.
