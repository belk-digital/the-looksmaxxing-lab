Comprehensive Product Details

CJC-1295 — GHRH Analog Molecular Profile
CJC-1295 is a synthetic analogue of growth hormone releasing hormone (GHRH), specifically a modified version of GHRH (1-29) — the biologically active amino-terminal fragment of the 44-amino acid endogenous GHRH. The modifications incorporated into CJC-1295 relative to native GHRH (1-29) increase metabolic stability by substituting dipeptides susceptible to DPP-IV (dipeptidyl peptidase-4) cleavage, extending the plasma half-life of the compound compared to native GHRH. The Drug Affinity Complex (DAC) version of CJC-1295 further extends this stability by incorporating a lysine-maleimide linkage that enables covalent binding to circulating albumin, dramatically extending the biological half-life in in vivo research models.
CJC-1295 acts at pituitary GHRH receptors (GHRHR) to stimulate growth hormone secretion via Gs protein coupling and adenylate cyclase activation. The resulting increase in intracellular cAMP promotes GH synthesis and pulsatile release from anterior pituitary somatotrophs. This GHRH-mimetic mechanism is distinct from ghrelin receptor-mediated GH secretion and provides a specific pharmacological tool for dissecting the GHRH-dependent arm of somatotropic axis regulation in research models.

Ipamorelin — Ghrelin Receptor Agonist Profile
Ipamorelin is a pentapeptide (Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂) with a molecular weight of 711.9 g/mol and a highly selective agonist profile at the growth hormone secretagogue receptor (GHSR-1a) — the canonical ghrelin receptor. Originally developed as a selective GH secretagogue, Ipamorelin is distinguished from earlier GHSR agonists (GHRP-2, GHRP-6) by its minimal off-target activity at corticotropin (ACTH/cortisol) and prolactin axes, making it a pharmacologically cleaner tool compound for isolating GH-specific GHSR-mediated signaling in research designs.
At GHSR-1a, Ipamorelin activates Gq/11 protein signaling, mobilizing intracellular calcium and activating PKC-dependent pathways that stimulate GH release from pituitary somatotrophs. This mechanism is synergistic with but independent of GHRH receptor signaling — the two pathways converge on GH secretion from somatotrophs through distinct intracellular second messenger systems. This receptor independence is the mechanistic basis for the research rationale of studying the CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin combination.

Why CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin Are Studied Together
The combination of CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin in a single research blend reflects a specific dual-mechanism hypothesis in growth hormone biology: that simultaneous activation of the GHRH receptor and the ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a) produces synergistic GH secretion exceeding what either compound achieves alone. Both receptors are expressed on anterior pituitary somatotrophs and stimulate GH release via distinct signaling pathways — cAMP (GHRHR) and intracellular calcium/PKC (GHSR-1a) — whose convergence on GH exocytosis may produce additive or synergistic effects in cell culture and preclinical animal model experimental systems.

Advanced Research Focus

Growth Hormone & IGF-1 Signaling Research
Research applications for CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin 10mg center on their utility as dual-mechanism pharmacological probes for the hypothalamic-pituitary somatotropic axis. The primary downstream consequence of combined GHRH receptor and GHSR agonism in experimental systems is stimulation of growth hormone secretion from pituitary somatotrophs — a response that initiates the GH/IGF-1 signaling cascade in peripheral tissues.
IGF-1 (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1) is the primary hepatic mediator of downstream growth hormone signaling. Pituitary GH release stimulates hepatic IGF-1 synthesis and secretion, which in turn exerts anabolic effects on protein synthesis, lipid metabolism, and cellular proliferation across peripheral tissues. Researchers studying the GH/IGF-1 axis — including the regulation of IGF-1 bioavailability by insulin-like growth factor binding proteins (IGFBPs), the receptor-mediated tissue-specific effects of IGF-1, and the feedback regulation of GH release — use GHRH analog and GHSR agonist compounds to modulate the upstream somatotropic axis in cell culture and preclinical animal model systems.
Comparative pharmacology studies examining CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin individually versus in combination — with or without the DAC modification on CJC-1295 — provide experimental data on the receptor crosstalk and synergy dynamics of dual-mechanism GH secretagogue approaches. Researchers also use CJC-1295 as a comparator against earlier GHRH analogs such as Sermorelin in studies examining GHRH receptor binding kinetics and downstream signaling amplitude.

Endocrine Axis & Recovery Biology Research
Beyond their role as GH secretagogue research tools, CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin have been applied in preclinical models investigating exercise physiology, tissue repair biology, and metabolic regulation. Growth hormone and its downstream mediator IGF-1 play central roles in muscle protein synthesis signaling (PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway), adipocyte lipolysis regulation, and connective tissue matrix maintenance. Preclinical research using GHS compounds as GH-stimulating agents has examined the downstream effects on nitrogen retention, fat oxidation markers, and collagen synthesis in animal models under controlled experimental conditions.
Sleep physiology research represents a distinct GHS research territory. Growth hormone secretion in vivo occurs in pulses — the largest and most consistent of which occurs during deep sleep (slow-wave sleep, SWS). Ipamorelin's selectivity for the GHSR-1a without significant cortisol or prolactin co-stimulation has made it a tool compound in preclinical studies examining the relationship between GHSR activation and sleep-associated GH pulse amplitude, distinct from GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 which confound these studies with off-target hormonal effects.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What is CJC-1295?
CJC-1295 is a synthetic analogue of growth hormone releasing hormone (GHRH) — specifically a stabilized modification of GHRH (1-29), the biologically active amino-terminal fragment of endogenous GHRH. It acts at pituitary GHRH receptors (GHRHR) to stimulate growth hormone secretion via Gs protein and cAMP signaling. Modified residues in its sequence increase resistance to DPP-IV enzymatic cleavage, extending stability compared to native GHRH. The DAC (Drug Affinity Complex) variant further extends half-life through albumin-binding chemistry. CJC-1295 is classified Research Use Only (RUO).
Q2: What is Ipamorelin?
Ipamorelin (Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂) is a selective pentapeptide agonist at the growth hormone secretagogue receptor (GHSR-1a) — the canonical ghrelin receptor on anterior pituitary somatotrophs. With a molecular weight of 711.9 g/mol, it stimulates GH release via Gq protein, intracellular calcium mobilization, and PKC signaling. Unlike earlier GHSR agonists (GHRP-2, GHRP-6), Ipamorelin demonstrates minimal off-target activity at ACTH/cortisol and prolactin axes, making it a pharmacologically selective research tool for GHSR-mediated GH biology studies.
Q3: What is a growth hormone secretagogue (GHS)?
A growth hormone secretagogue (GHS) is a compound that stimulates growth hormone (GH) secretion from pituitary somatotrophs through endogenous receptor signaling pathways, as distinct from exogenous recombinant GH administration. GHS compounds target two primary receptors: the GHRH receptor (GHRHR), activated by GHRH and GHRH analogs like CJC-1295; and the ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a), activated by ghrelin and ghrelin mimetics like Ipamorelin. Both pathways converge on GH secretion from pituitary somatotrophs via distinct intracellular signaling mechanisms.
Q4: Why are CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin studied in combination?
CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin target different GH-stimulating receptors — GHRHR and GHSR-1a respectively — that signal through distinct intracellular pathways (cAMP vs. Gq/Ca2+). Because these pathways are mechanistically independent but converge on GH secretion from the same cell type (pituitary somatotrophs), researchers hypothesize and study synergistic GH secretory responses when both are applied simultaneously. The combination design also allows researchers to compare individual receptor contributions to total GH output against the combined dual-receptor response in the same experimental system.
Q5: What is GHRH and what role does it play in growth hormone biology?
Growth hormone releasing hormone (GHRH) is a 44-amino acid hypothalamic peptide that binds GHRH receptors on pituitary somatotrophs to stimulate growth hormone synthesis and pulsatile release. It is one of two primary physiological signals governing GH secretion — the other being ghrelin, which acts through GHSR-1a. GHRH is released from the arcuate nucleus of the hypothalamus, travels via the hypophyseal portal system to the anterior pituitary, and activates adenylate cyclase, increasing intracellular cAMP and GH exocytosis. CJC-1295 is designed to mimic and extend the biological activity of endogenous GHRH.
Q6: What are CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin research applications?
Research applications include: dual-mechanism GH secretagogue pharmacology assays, GHRH receptor vs. GHSR-1a synergy studies, GH/IGF-1 signaling axis research in cell culture and preclinical models, IGF-1 bioavailability and IGFBP regulation studies, comparative GHRH analog pharmacology (CJC-1295 vs. Sermorelin), sleep-associated GH pulse biology research using Ipamorelin's selective GHSR profile, metabolic and body composition pathway research in preclinical animal models, and receptor selectivity profiling comparing GHSR agonists.
Q7: What is the difference between CJC-1295 and Sermorelin?
CJC-1295 and Sermorelin are both synthetic GHRH analogs, but differ in structure and stability. Sermorelin is GHRH (1-29) without additional stabilizing modifications — it has a shorter half-life due to DPP-IV susceptibility. CJC-1295 incorporates modified residues that confer DPP-IV resistance and extended plasma half-life, and the DAC version adds albumin-binding chemistry for further half-life extension. In research applications requiring extended receptor exposure or longer pulse modeling, CJC-1295 (with DAC) offers a longer-acting pharmacological profile compared to Sermorelin.
