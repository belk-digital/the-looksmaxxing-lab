Comprehensive Product Details

Molecular Characteristics & Biochemical Role
Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide (NAD+) is a dinucleotide composed of adenine and nicotinamide nucleotides joined by a phosphoanhydride bridge. With a molecular weight of 663.43 g/mol and CAS number 53-84-9, it exists in two interconvertible redox states: the oxidized form (NAD+) and the reduced form (NADH). This redox cycling underpins its central biochemical function as an electron shuttle across the mitochondrial electron transport chain and a broad range of cytosolic metabolic reactions.
In research settings, NAD+ is valued for its role as both a coenzyme and a signaling molecule. Its dual function — driving redox chemistry while simultaneously acting as a substrate for regulatory enzymes including sirtuins, PARPs, and CD38 — makes it an exceptionally versatile substrate for studies spanning cellular energy metabolism, genomic stability, and chronobiological research.

NAD+ in Cellular Metabolism
The biochemical significance of NAD+ in cellular metabolism extends across glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation. In each pathway, NAD+ accepts hydride ions to become NADH, which subsequently donates electrons at Complex I of the mitochondrial respiratory chain — a step directly linked to ATP synthesis and cellular energy output.
Beyond bioenergetics, the NAD+/NADH ratio functions as a metabolic sensor that modulates gene expression, mitochondrial biogenesis signaling, and cellular stress response pathways. Research exploring this ratio as a readout of metabolic health or as a target for experimental intervention benefits from a well-characterized, high-purity NAD+ preparation as a starting substrate.

  SECTION 10 — Product FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What is NAD+?
NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every living cell and one of the most fundamental molecules in biochemistry. It functions primarily as an electron carrier in cellular respiration — accepting and donating electrons across the glycolytic, citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation pathways. It also serves as a substrate for regulatory enzymes including sirtuins, PARPs, and CD38 that govern cellular repair, gene expression, and stress response signaling. In research settings, NAD+ 500mg is used as a high-purity substrate for investigating these pathways in controlled experimental systems.
Q2: How does NAD+ work at a cellular level?
NAD+ works by accepting hydride ions (H⁻) during metabolic reactions, becoming NADH. NADH subsequently donates electrons at Complex I of the mitochondrial electron transport chain, driving proton pumping and ATP synthesis. Beyond this bioenergetic function, NAD+ is the obligate substrate for sirtuin deacylase enzymes, which require NAD+ to remove acetyl and acyl groups from histone and non-histone proteins — directly linking cellular metabolic status to epigenetic regulation. The cellular NAD+/NADH ratio is therefore both a product and a regulator of metabolic activity.
Q3: Why is NAD+ important in longevity research?
NAD+ occupies a central position in longevity research because its intracellular concentration declines with age in multiple model organisms, and this decline correlates with reduced sirtuin activity, impaired mitochondrial function, and diminished DNA repair capacity. Researchers investigating the mechanisms of biological aging use NAD+ preparations to study sirtuin pathway activation, the role of NAD+ pool size in mitochondrial biogenesis, and the relationship between PARP enzyme activity and genomic stability. NAD+ 500mg from The Looksmaxxing Lab provides a verified-purity substrate suitable for these experimental systems.
Q4: What role does NAD+ play in mitochondria?
Within mitochondria, NAD+ is a critical component of the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and the electron transport chain. NAD+-dependent dehydrogenases — including isocitrate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, and malate dehydrogenase — convert NAD+ to NADH at multiple steps of the TCA cycle. The accumulated NADH then delivers electrons to Complex I of the respiratory chain, initiating the proton gradient that drives ATP synthase. NAD+ is also involved in regulating mitochondrial sirtuin activity (particularly SIRT3), linking energy status to organelle quality control and protein deacetylation.
Q5: What are the research applications for NAD+ 500mg?
Research applications include cellular bioenergetics assays, mitochondrial respiration studies, sirtuin enzyme kinetics, PARP activity and DNA repair modeling, NAD+ pool quantification experiments, longevity pathway research, and metabolic flux analysis. The compound is also used as a reference standard in assay development and as an experimental substrate in cell-free biochemical systems. Its broad biochemical relevance makes it applicable across multiple research disciplines including molecular biology, biochemistry, geroscience, and metabolic medicine research.

Q8: How should NAD+ 500mg be stored?
Lyophilized NAD+ 500mg should be stored at –20°C in a dry environment, protected from moisture and light. The lyophilized form offers superior stability over dissolved preparations and is suitable for long-term laboratory storage under these conditions. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles. Once reconstituted in sterile water or phosphate-buffered saline (pH 7.0–7.4), the solution should be used promptly or stored at 4°C and used within 24–48 hours, consistent with standard research compound handling protocols.
Q9: What is the difference between NAD+ and NMN?
NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) is the biologically active coenzyme directly involved in cellular redox reactions and enzyme substrate functions. NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide) is a precursor molecule that cells convert to NAD+ via enzymatic synthesis. In research applications, NAD+ is used when direct substrate availability is required — such as enzyme kinetics assays or cell-free biochemical systems. NMN is typically studied as an NAD+ precursor in cellular uptake and biosynthesis pathway research. The two compounds are not interchangeable as research substrates.
