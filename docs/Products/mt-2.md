Comprehensive Product Details

Molecular Characteristics & Peptide Structure
MT-2 (Melanotan 2) is a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide with the amino acid sequence Ac-Nle-c[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-NH₂. It was developed as a metabolically stable analogue of alpha-melanocyte stimulating hormone (α-MSH) — the endogenous peptide that activates melanocortin receptors in skin, the central nervous system, and peripheral tissues. The substitution of norleucine (Nle) for methionine and the incorporation of D-phenylalanine (D-Phe) increase receptor binding affinity and resistance to enzymatic degradation relative to native α-MSH, making MT-2 a higher-potency research probe for melanocortin receptor biology.
With a molecular weight of approximately 1024.2 g/mol, MT-2 is presented in lyophilized form to ensure stability during storage and transport. The cyclic structure confers additional conformational stability, reducing peptide bond hydrolysis relative to linear α-MSH analogues. This structural resilience is particularly relevant for researchers working with extended in vitro assay timelines or performing comparative receptor activation studies.

Melanocortin Receptor Activity Profile
MT-2 exhibits agonist activity at multiple melanocortin receptor subtypes (MCR). It binds with high affinity to MC1R — the primary receptor mediating melanogenesis and skin pigmentation responses — and demonstrates significant activity at MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R, which are expressed in the central nervous system and peripheral tissues. This broad receptor profile makes MT-2 a versatile experimental compound for researchers investigating the melanocortin system across multiple physiological contexts.
MC4R engagement is of particular interest in neuroendocrine research, as this receptor subtype is expressed in hypothalamic nuclei associated with energy homeostasis, appetite regulation, and autonomic nervous system output. Researchers studying the downstream consequences of MC4R activation — including cAMP signaling cascades and downstream gene transcription — benefit from MT-2's well-documented receptor binding profile in the published literature.

Advanced Research Focus

Pigmentation Biology & MC1R Receptor Research
The primary research application driving interest in MT-2 is its role as a pharmacological probe for melanocortin receptor-mediated pigmentation pathways. MC1R is the canonical receptor governing eumelanin synthesis in melanocytes — its activation by α-MSH analogues initiates a cAMP/PKA signaling cascade that upregulates MITF (microphthalmia-associated transcription factor), which in turn drives transcription of melanogenic enzymes including tyrosinase, TRP-1, and DCT.
Researchers investigating the molecular regulation of melanogenesis, the role of MC1R variants in pigmentation phenotype variation, or the signaling crosstalk between melanocortin and other receptor systems use MT-2 as an experimental agonist in primary melanocyte cultures, melanoma cell lines, and reconstituted skin models. Its high MC1R affinity and enzymatic stability relative to native α-MSH make it a preferred tool compound for these in vitro applications.
Comparative receptor pharmacology studies — contrasting MT-2 with Melanotan 1 (afamelanotide), which is more MC1R-selective, or with endogenous α-MSH — benefit from MT-2's documented multi-receptor activity profile. Researchers designing dose-response assays or receptor selectivity studies will find MT-2's published Kd values and receptor binding constants well-characterized in the primary melanocortin pharmacology literature.

Neuroendocrine & Energy Regulation Research
Beyond its canonical pigmentation applications, MT-2's activity at MC4R has made it a relevant compound in preclinical research examining hypothalamic energy homeostasis signaling. MC4R is highly expressed in the paraventricular nucleus and arcuate nucleus of the hypothalamus, where it participates in regulating food intake, thermogenesis, and sympathetic nervous system output in rodent models. MT-2 has been used as an experimental MC4R agonist in preclinical in vivo and ex vivo studies examining these pathways.
Researchers modeling the interplay between peripheral melanocortin signaling and central neuroendocrine function use MT-2 as a tool to probe receptor-dependent versus receptor-independent effects in comparative pharmacology designs. Its multi-receptor activity necessitates careful experimental controls — particularly when studying MC4R-specific downstream effects — but simultaneously allows the same compound to be applied across multiple research questions within a single study.

  SECTION 10 — Product FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What is MT-2 (Melanotan 2)?
MT-2, also known as Melanotan 2 or MT-II, is a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide analogue of alpha-melanocyte stimulating hormone (α-MSH) — an endogenous neuropeptide that activates melanocortin receptors. Developed as a metabolically stable, higher-potency α-MSH analogue, MT-2 is used in laboratory research to study melanocortin receptor signaling, pigmentation biology, and related neuroendocrine pathways. It is classified as a Research Use Only (RUO) compound and is not approved for human or animal therapeutic use.
Q2: How does MT-2 work?
MT-2 works by binding to and activating melanocortin receptors — primarily MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R. Receptor activation triggers intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP) signaling cascades that vary in downstream effect by receptor subtype and tissue location. MC1R activation in melanocytes initiates the melanogenic enzyme cascade (tyrosinase, TRP-1, DCT) through upregulation of MITF, the master regulator of melanin production. MC4R activation in the hypothalamus engages neuroendocrine signaling pathways associated with energy homeostasis and autonomic regulation in preclinical research models.
Q3: What is the melanocortin system?
The melanocortin system is a network of G protein-coupled receptors (MC1R through MC5R), endogenous peptide ligands (α-MSH, β-MSH, γ-MSH, ACTH), and endogenous antagonists (AgRP, ASIP) that regulate diverse physiological processes including skin pigmentation, energy balance, inflammation modulation, exocrine gland function, and sexual function. These receptors share structural homology and are activated by peptides derived from the proopiomelanocortin (POMC) gene. MT-2 acts as a synthetic non-selective agonist for this receptor family and is used as a research tool to probe its function.
Q4: What receptors does MT-2 target?
MT-2 targets four melanocortin receptor subtypes: MC1R (melanocytes — pigmentation), MC3R (hypothalamus, adipose tissue — energy balance and inflammation research), MC4R (hypothalamus — energy homeostasis and neuroendocrine regulation), and MC5R (exocrine glands — peripheral tissue research). All four are class A G protein-coupled receptors that signal primarily through adenylate cyclase and cAMP elevation. MT-2's multi-receptor activity profile distinguishes it from more selective analogues such as Melanotan 1 (afamelanotide), which preferentially binds MC1R.
Q5: What are MT-2's research applications?
Research applications for MT-2 include: in vitro melanogenesis studies using primary melanocyte cultures or melanoma cell lines, MC1R receptor pharmacology and binding assays, MC4R-mediated neuroendocrine signaling research in preclinical models, comparative melanocortin receptor pharmacology (MT-2 vs afamelanotide vs α-MSH), pigmentation biology and MITF transcription factor research, and cAMP second messenger pathway analysis in MCR-expressing cell systems. Its broad receptor activity and published pharmacological profile make it a well-characterized experimental probe across these disciplines.
Q6: What is the difference between MT-2 and Melanotan 1?
MT-2 (Melanotan 2) and Melanotan 1 (afamelanotide) are both synthetic α-MSH analogues, but they differ in receptor selectivity and peptide structure. MT-2 is a cyclic heptapeptide with broad activity across MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R. Melanotan 1 is a linear tridecapeptide with greater MC1R selectivity and reduced MC4R activity. In research settings, MT-2 is preferred when multi-receptor melanocortin system studies are required, while Melanotan 1 is the preferred tool compound for MC1R-specific pigmentation research with reduced off-target neuroendocrine effects.
Q7: How is MT-2 purity verified?
Every batch of MT-2 10mg is analyzed by an independent third-party laboratory using HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography) prior to release. HPLC separates and quantifies the target compound against co-eluting impurities, truncated peptide sequences, synthesis residues, and degradation products. We guarantee a minimum purity of ≥99%. The full chromatogram, purity percentage, testing methodology, testing date, and certifying laboratory name are documented in the Certificate of Analysis included with every order.
Q8: What is included in the Certificate of Analysis?
The Certificate of Analysis (COA) for each MT-2 batch includes: compound identity confirmation (Melanotan 2 / MT-II), the unique batch lot number, HPLC purity percentage and chromatogram, molecular weight confirmation, testing methodology, testing date, and the name of the independent certifying laboratory. This documentation provides complete batch traceability for institutional compliance records, grant documentation, and peer-reviewed publication methodology sections.
Q9: How should MT-2 10mg be stored?
Lyophilized MT-2 10mg should be stored at –20°C in a dry environment, protected from moisture and light exposure. The lyophilized cyclic peptide form offers excellent long-term stability under these conditions. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles, as thermal cycling can degrade peptide integrity over time. Once reconstituted in bacteriostatic water or sterile phosphate-buffered saline, the solution should be stored at 4°C and used within 7 days, consistent with standard research peptide handling protocols.
