Comprehensive Product Details

Molecular Characteristics & Tetrapeptide Structure
Epithalon is a synthetic tetrapeptide with the amino acid sequence Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (AEDG) and a molecular weight of 390.35 g/mol. It was developed by Professor Vladimir Khavinson and colleagues at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology as a synthetic analogue of Epithalamin — an endogenous polypeptide complex extracted from bovine pineal gland tissue that had demonstrated bioregulatory activity in aging animal models in earlier Soviet-era research programs. The synthetic tetrapeptide replicates the proposed bioactive core of this endogenous compound.
As a four-amino-acid sequence, Epithalon occupies a distinct structural category from longer research peptides: its small size confers high aqueous solubility, metabolic stability relative to longer chains, and ease of reconstitution in standard research buffers. Presented as a lyophilized powder, each 10mg batch maintains molecular integrity through long-term cold storage and is ready for reconstitution immediately prior to in vitro experimental application.

Telomere & Telomerase Biology Context
The primary biological context driving research interest in Epithalon is its reported interaction with telomere homeostasis — specifically its putative capacity to stimulate telomerase (hTERT) expression and activity in somatic cell models. Telomeres are repetitive DNA sequences (TTAGGG repeats) that cap the ends of linear chromosomes and shorten with each cell division. Progressive telomere attrition is a recognized molecular marker of cellular aging and contributes to replicative senescence — the state in which cells permanently exit the cell cycle.
Telomerase is the ribonucleoprotein enzyme that counteracts telomere shortening by adding TTAGGG repeats to chromosome ends. Its activity is high in germ cells and stem cells but is largely suppressed in most somatic tissues. Research examining peptides that may modulate telomerase expression in vitro is an active area of molecular geroscience. Epithalon's reported effects on hTERT gene expression and telomere length maintenance in cell culture models — published in journals including Bulletin of Experimental Biology and Medicine — have made it one of the most studied synthetic peptides in the longevity research space.

Advanced Research Focus

Cellular Aging & Senescence Research Applications
The primary research applications for Epithalon 10mg center on its utility as a tool compound in cellular aging biology, telomere homeostasis research, and senescence pathway investigation. In vitro applications include: assays measuring hTERT gene expression changes in response to peptide treatment in primary human fibroblast or epithelial cell cultures, telomere length quantification assays (q-FISH, q-PCR-based methods) comparing treated and untreated cell populations, and senescence-associated beta-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining studies examining whether peptide treatment alters the rate of senescence onset in serially passaged cell lines.
Researchers modeling the relationship between oxidative stress and telomere attrition have also used Epithalon in assay designs examining whether antioxidant or cytoprotective peptide signaling can mitigate oxidative damage-induced telomere shortening. This application sits at the intersection of geroscience, molecular biology, and peptide pharmacology — a convergence that makes Epithalon relevant across multiple research disciplines beyond its immediate telomere biology context.
Comparative pharmacology studies contrasting Epithalon with other longevity-associated research peptides — such as SS-31 (Szeto-Schiller peptide for mitochondrial research) or MOTS-C (mitochondria-derived peptide) — benefit from Epithalon's well-documented preclinical publication record and characterized research profile. Pineal Gland Biology & Neuroendocrine Research

The pineal gland produces melatonin and a range of bioregulatory peptides that exert systemic effects on circadian rhythm entrainment, immune function, and hypothalamic-pituitary axis regulation. Declining pineal gland secretory activity with age has been proposed in the bioregulation literature as a contributing factor to age-associated physiological changes in multiple organ systems.
Researchers studying the neuroendocrine aspects of biological aging — including the role of pineal-derived bioregulators in immune senescence, circadian rhythm disruption, and hormonal axis dysregulation — use Epithalon as a defined synthetic compound with a characterized relationship to the pineal regulatory peptide family. This allows investigators to study pineal bioregulatory biology with a purity-verified, batch-traceable research material rather than crude gland extracts, improving experimental reproducibility and data quality.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What is Epithalon (Epitalon)?
Epithalon (also spelled Epitalon) is a synthetic tetrapeptide with the amino acid sequence Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (AEDG) and a molecular weight of 390.35 g/mol. It was developed by Professor Vladimir Khavinson at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology as a synthetic analogue of Epithalamin, an endogenous polypeptide extracted from bovine pineal gland tissue. In research settings, Epithalon is studied for its reported effects on telomerase expression, telomere homeostasis, and age-associated cellular biology. It is classified Research Use Only (RUO) and is not intended for human therapeutic use.
Q2: What are telomeres, and why are they relevant to Epithalon research?
Telomeres are repetitive DNA sequences (TTAGGG repeats) that cap and protect the ends of linear chromosomes. They shorten with each somatic cell division — a process linked to cellular senescence and biological aging. When telomeres reach a critically short length, cells permanently exit the cell cycle, a state called replicative senescence. Epithalon is studied in the research context of telomere biology because preclinical publications have reported effects on telomerase (hTERT) expression in cell culture models — making it a tool compound for in vitro investigation of telomere homeostasis pathways.
Q3: What is telomerase, and what is its role in Epithalon research?
Telomerase is a ribonucleoprotein enzyme that extends telomere length by adding TTAGGG repeat sequences to chromosome ends. Its catalytic subunit (hTERT) is highly expressed in germ cells, stem cells, and certain cancer cell types, but is largely suppressed in most differentiated somatic tissues. This suppression is a key driver of replicative senescence. Epithalon has been studied in cell culture models for its reported capacity to stimulate hTERT gene expression, making it a research tool for investigating pharmacological modulation of telomerase activity in somatic cell systems.
Q4: What are Epithalon's research applications?
Research applications include: hTERT gene expression assays, telomere length quantification studies (q-FISH, q-PCR methods), senescence-associated beta-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining experiments, oxidative stress and telomere attrition interaction studies, pineal gland bioregulation research, neuroendocrine aging biology investigation, and comparative longevity peptide pharmacology studies. Epithalon's well-characterized publication record in the geroscience literature makes it a suitable reference compound for investigators designing multi-peptide comparative experimental systems.
Q5: What makes Epithalon unique compared to other longevity research peptides?
Epithalon is unique in three respects: its pineal gland origin (as a synthetic analogue of Epithalamin, a pineal-derived bioregulatory polypeptide), its tetrapeptide size (four amino acids — among the smallest research peptides with a characterized publication record), and its specific research context in telomere biology and telomerase activation in somatic cell models. Unlike mitochondria-targeted longevity peptides (SS-31, MOTS-C) or NAD+-pathway compounds, Epithalon's primary mechanistic research territory is nuclear — chromosomal end protection and replicative capacity maintenance.
Q6: How does Epithalon work in research models?
In published in vitro and preclinical research, Epithalon has been reported to upregulate hTERT (human telomerase reverse transcriptase) gene expression in somatic cell cultures, correlating with telomere length preservation and delayed onset of replicative senescence in serial passage experiments. Proposed mechanisms include direct interaction with chromatin regulatory elements upstream of the hTERT promoter, and indirect effects through modulation of pineal-related neuroendocrine signaling pathways. The precise receptor or binding partner mediating these effects in cell-free systems remains under investigation.

Q9: How should Epithalon 10mg be stored?
Lyophilized Epithalon 10mg should be stored at –20°C in a dry environment, protected from moisture and light.
